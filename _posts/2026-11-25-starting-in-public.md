For a little while, my tech blog was live and the homepage said “No posts yet.” I had a place to write, which was something I’d wanted for a while. Now I had to decide what to say.

## What got me moving

I read something recently that stuck with me. When you feel stuck, putting something out in public can help you build momentum.

When I’m stuck, I usually spend more time thinking. I’ll picture a product I could make, imagine how someone might use it, and start listing all the things I’d need to figure out. I enjoy that part. It’s also where an idea can sit for a long time.

Starting a blog gave me something small enough to finish. I could write one post about why I wanted to do this. I didn’t need a backlog of topics to find out whether I liked writing in public.

## The part I want to write down

I work as a data scientist, and I’m always thinking about products I could build. A question about the data can turn into an idea for a tool. Then I start wondering what somebody would click first, what they’d expect to happen, and whether I’ve given them enough information to make a decision.

I want to write about what happens between that first idea and something another person could use. When I do figure something out, I tend to move on to the next problem. A few weeks later I might remember what worked and forget why I spent so much time trying the other thing. That’s often the part I would’ve wanted to read if I were starting from scratch.

## What I hope to share

Some posts will be about products I’m building. Others might be a short note about a bug, a design decision, or a question I’m still working through. I’d like to get in the habit of writing those things down while I still remember what made them hard.

I’m glad the first thing to take the place of “No posts yet” is the reason I started the blog. Now I’ve got somewhere to put the next thing I learn.
