Working through my L1 and L2 certifications gave me a way to make sense of the oil and gas companies around Houston. I started with a few company names and a question: what do these businesses actually do? Following a barrel from the well to a refinery helped me see where each company fits and what someone working there needs to know.

## Upstream: getting oil and gas out of the ground

Upstream is where companies look for oil and gas, drill wells, and bring them into production. Oilfield services companies can support that work. Halliburton, for example, provides services for drilling and completing wells.

As a data scientist, I think about how noisy the evidence can be. If a well is producing less than expected, what would help someone figure out whether that’s a temporary change or a reason to take a closer look?

## Midstream: moving and storing it

Midstream made more sense to me once I pictured the handoff. Oil has to move from where it’s produced to where it can be processed or shipped. Pipelines and storage terminals help make that happen.

Enbridge’s Houston Oil Terminal gave me a local example. It’s part of a system that brings crude from northern Alberta to the U.S. Gulf Coast. The questions here are about timing. What’s coming in? How much room is available to store it? What changes if a shipment is late? [Enbridge’s infrastructure overview](https://www.enbridge.com/~/media/Enb/Documents/Factsheets/FS_EnergyInfrastructureAssets.pdf)

## Downstream: making products from crude

A refinery turns crude into products such as gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel. ExxonMobil’s Baytown refinery and Phillips 66’s Sweeny refinery gave me two examples in the Houston area. Here I start thinking about equipment and operating conditions. If a reading starts to drift, how would an operator tell whether it needs attention now or can wait for planned maintenance? [Greater Houston Partnership’s Houston Facts](https://wpb.houston.org/app/uploads/2025/09/Houston-Facts-2025-Publication.pdf)

What I took away from L1 and L2 is that the same barrel can be part of three very different workdays. At the well, someone is watching production. At a terminal, someone is making room for what’s coming in. At a refinery, someone is keeping a process running. Before I think about what I could build for any of them, I want to know what decision they’re trying to make.
